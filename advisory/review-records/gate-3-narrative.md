## Scope of this gate

The Narrative gate is the **line-by-line reading** of every word the player reads. This is the gate where §4.1.1 (named sacred figures in text) and §4.1.4 (false doctrinal claims) are most likely to be triggered, and where the **literary tone** the project rests on is set. Reviewers read each event and each ending in full and assess:

- Does any line name or allude to a figure on [`advisory/prohibited-names.txt`](../prohibited-names.txt)? (§4.1.1)
- Does any line present a Mahāyāna doctrinal position (on emptiness, buddha-nature, the two truths, etc.) as canonical teaching? (§4.1.4)
- Does any line allude to restricted tantric material? (§4.1.2)
- Does any line encode or imply a karma → identity causal claim in narrative form, even if the engine itself does not? (§4.1.3, narrative surface)
- Does the choice graph coerce the player into a "doctrinally correct" path? (advisory, §4.2 if stereotyping)
- Does the vocabulary lexicon's intended sense match how a reader would actually receive the line?

Per-reviewer remit at this gate:

- **R1** assesses Tang-era vocabulary, ritual texture, and social-institution plausibility in Life 1 events; checks the lexicon against historical usage.
- **R2** assesses each event and ending line for false-doctrinal-assertion risk (§4.1.4); this is the highest-effort review at this gate.
- **R3** assesses stereotyping and exoticization of Tang-era characters and of the fantasy setting's echoing of them.
- **R4** assesses whether each event and ending reads as respectful to a practicing lay household, or as something that would land badly in a sangha context.

Explicitly **out of scope** (panel.md §3): writing quality, branching-tree elegance, pacing (covered by playtest at Gate 6 / T33), difficulty.
