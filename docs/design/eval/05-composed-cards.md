# Composed cards by era (real path: operations.harvestWithFiller, live archive)

Five consecutive harvests per kind per era, each through the same filler
the player hits, with the archive carried between cards so title/detail
dedup operates as it does in play. Alternates long fire via the window
shape, not a generator flag.

## Tang (a market life)

### thing

1. **Folded measure** — A six-fold carpenter's rule of seasoned boxwood, hinged with flat brass pins that still snap into place.
   _a jointed boxwood rule_ (uncommon)
   Carved notches mark both the imperial foot and the shorter river pace used by boatwrights along the canal. When unfolded against rough-hewn timber, its straight edge cuts through dispute before the first saw-cut is made.

2. **The bowl on the sill** — A brown earthenware bowl kept by the pot and not in the cupboard, for whoever turns up after the household has eaten.
   _a spare earthenware bowl_ (common)
   You have washed it more times than you have used it, and you have never once considered putting it away.

3. **The bench chime** — A flat-rimmed bronze chime in washed fleece, and the only sound in the workshop that makes people stop.
   _a bronze workshop chime at rest_ (common)
   It is not tuned to anything you could name. Struck at the wrong moment it sounds like a question; struck at the right one it sounds like the end of one.

4. **Thumbed account** — A mulberry-paper account book gone soft at the corners, its columns still adding up after every hand that has turned it.
   _a stitched mulberry-paper account_ (common)
   Somebody before you wrote the totals in a careful, upright hand, made mistakes in the hundreds column, and ruled them out neatly. You inherited the ruling as much as the sum.

5. **Shared cloak** — A heavy wool cloak treated with mutton tallow against river sleet, hung by the latch for whoever leaves last.
   _a shared wool river-cloak_ (rare)
   It has warmed three apprentices and a stranded courier through the first freeze. You do not think of it as a garment. You think of it as the door's second argument against the cold. Four people have patched it, and no two patches are the same cloth.

### outcome

1. **His surname, at the lock** — A wandering cooper, greeted by name before he could unroll the permit onto the damp stone.
   _a traveler remembered at the lock_ (uncommon)
   You said his surname and his home village and the line of porters passed it along behind him like dry kindling catching. By the time the constable got to him he was ordinary business, and nobody looked up twice.

2. **The hat on the flagstones** — A bamboo hat set down steaming on cold stone, and a man who had walked here from the pass.
   _a traveler fed by the stove_ (common)
   He left the hat where he dropped it and picked it up again when he was ready to go, and in between he said almost nothing, and you did not make him. The brazier is still warm.

3. **A storm that missed** — Black thunderheads split over the southern ridge, dumping their hail onto bare gravel slopes instead of the barley.
   _a summer gale that broke elsewhere_ (common)
   The wind smelled of lightning and torn pine boughs all afternoon while you lashed straw mats over the drying sheds. When the gale broke it sheared east along the river gorges, leaving only cool drops on the hot tiles and the ditches running clear.

4. **A debt settled** — A double brush-stroke of vermilion ink drawn across the tavern tab, closing an obligation from the flood year.
   _a struck ledger account_ (common)
   Three jars of fermented bean paste and an afternoon spent re-shingling the oil press settled what cash could not. The innkeeper nodded without looking up from his abacus, folding the receipt slip into your sleeve.

5. **On the warm kiln bricks** — A scarred yellow cat, three days on the perimeter wall, and then a decision.
   _a stray settling by the kiln_ (rare)
   It drank the skimmed whey and went straight back up to the bricks it had been watching from, and it has been there every night since. Nobody in this house agreed to feed it. It never asked anyone to. It has stopped being a visitor, which is a different thing entirely.

### change

1. **The ping before the fracture** — Stressed iron announces itself a good half-mile before it lets go, if anyone is listening.
   _an ear tuned to strained metal_ (uncommon)
   You hear it now in a way you could not before, and it is not magic and it is not a gift. It is four years of stopping to look at the thing everyone else walked past, which turns out to be the whole mechanism.

2. **The roads you never took** — Every mile of the crossing you worried about, and none of the miles you walked.
   _a shed weight on the mountain road_ (common)
   The roads were not what made the pack heavy. You know that now, and you have not entirely forgiven the roads, and you have stopped packing for them.

3. **You look for a second cup** — Your hand draws two celadon cups from the drying rack every time the kettle comes to a boil.
   _setting out two cups by instinct_ (common)
   Even when the workshop is empty at twilight, you wipe the rim of the second cup and place it opposite your bench. Someone almost always pushes the screen aside before the steam stops rising, as if drawn by the spare bowl.

4. **A slower morning** — You lay out your tools and brew a bowl of tea before the morning crowd starts shouting in the lane.
   _a deliberate start to the day_ (common)
   Instead of tumbling into the alley at the first market drum, you trim the lamp and check every strap on your carrying basket. Taking that quiet half-hour to sit with hot tea prevents hasty packing and keeps fragile goods from spilling later on the road.

5. **Across the gravel, again** — Your feet arrive at the joiner's bench some seconds before you have decided anything.
   _an unthinking return to work_ (rare)
   The bell no longer catches you hesitating at the threshold. You are through the gate, across the gravel, and standing at the bench with your hand already on a blade before the thought of the day has finished forming. A practice this old stops being a decision some weeks in, and starts being a thing that would have to be stopped deliberately.

### person

1. **Auntie Qian the keyholder** — The heavy latch-key hangs beside the dried peppers, and it is not for the door it opens.
   _a neighbor holding a key_ (uncommon)
   She holds the key for four households on the lane, and the argument about whose turn it is to be given it is an old one and is settled annually, in the same way, in the same week.

2. **Samantabhadra (Puxian)** — He turns up where the digging is and does the part nobody wants, and he is enormous.
   _the heavy labor carried through_ (common)
   He steadies a post while others lift, and when they set it down he is the one who is standing in the hole they dug, holding it true, waiting for the next timber.

3. **Amitābha** — He extends an open hand westward each dusk as the day's labor closes.
   _the welcoming light of the west_ (common)
   At sundown when the market drum signals closing, his name passes among tired laborers packing their stalls. He leans outward from the western sky with an open palm, taking in every weary call without asking for credentials.

4. **Bhaiṣajyaguru, the Medicine Buddha** — He offers bitter herbs and clean water to whoever is burning with fever.
   _the healer with the medicine bowl_ (common)
   He sits beside the sickbed with an iron bowl of steeped myrobalan, cooling hot foreheads through the midnight watch. His twelve vows answer common aches directly, dispensing bitter draughts without asking what copper is in your purse.

5. **Maitreya** — He watches the carts come through the south gate and has been ready for a very long time.
   _the coming teacher keeping watch_ (rare)
   At the crossroads where the three roads meet, a man stops to ask the way. Maitreya describes the turn exactly, at length, and the man thanks him and takes it. The man came back a year later from the same crossing and asked the same question.

### place

1. **Wutai Shan** — The northern mountain revered as Mañjuśrī's seat.
   _a mountain of wisdom_ (uncommon)
   Pilgrims climb past terraces where the sword is said to have been seen. The cold is part of the teaching, the way the climb is part of the arrival.

2. **The clock attic** — A timber loft smelling of pine pitch where copper clepsydras and bronze gears measure the watches of the night.
   _a water-clock loft in the watch tower_ (common)
   Bronze floats rise in tiered reservoirs of siphoned well water, tripping counterweights that strike small gongs on the quarter-hour. The astronomer's apprentice sleeps on an oat-straw pallet between the copper drums, waking only to refill the upper basin.

3. **Jiuhua Shan** — The southern mountain of Kṣitigarbha's great vow.
   _a mountain of the vow_ (common)
   Mist, stone steps, and a bell that the visitor from Korea is said to have rung first. The ground holds the promise longer than the season.

4. **The dry cistern** — A vaulted brick chamber beneath the garrison storehouse, dry for years but still cool in the summer heat.
   _a dry reservoir under the garrison_ (common)
   Faint mineral waterlines still ring the curved brick walls where rain cisterns once filled. Down here the street clamor and market alarms fade into damp silence, leaving a quiet refuge when the garrison gates swing shut.

5. **The extra seat** — A cedar bench with a clean bowl and chopsticks, kept vacant at every evening meal for an unexpected traveler.
   _a place set for an unexpected guest_ (uncommon)
   It sits at the corner nearest the courtyard door, swept clear and laid with fresh willow chopsticks before the soup pot is ladled. The children know never to heap winter cloaks there, keeping the threshold clear for whoever steps in out of the dark.

## The Garden of Arrivals

### thing

1. **Washed fleece cushion** — A bench pad soaked and dried until it holds warmth and gives none.
   _the bench pad by the outer gate_ (uncommon)
   The fleece smells of slow water and nothing else, which is the point of it.

2. **Braided vow-cord** — Three hemp strands round one wire, notched for counting laps between the basins.
   _the cord the vow-walkers carry_ (common)
   The notches are worn round and the fifth is black, and you can feel how many times it has been gripped.

3. **Water-slate stylus** — A river-bone stylus for writing on wet slate that will not wait for you.
   _the attendant's river-bone stylus_ (common)
   The flax is greasy enough to turn in your hand, and everything you mark blurs the moment you lift the stone away.

4. **Quiet instrument** — A cast-bronze chime resting in washed fleece on the bench, waiting to strike the change of the midday shift.
   _a bronze workshop chime at rest_ (common)
   A bowl-rung of fired clay, hung on a silk cord above the sleeping quarters; struck once it stops an argument that would otherwise run until morning.

5. **Storm-reed whistle** — A plugged reed on a lanyard, for warning the court of the storm-edge.
   _the post whistle_ (rare)
   You put it to your mouth and the wax plug makes a low, closed sound that carries further than a shout would. The reed is split a hairline down one side, and it still carries.

### outcome

1. **The permit, unrolled for nothing** — A document that was never needed, and a man who did not have to explain himself.
   _a traveler remembered at the lock_ (uncommon)
   He kept it in his hand a while after, folded again, and then he did not use it, and you understood that this had not been a courtesy to him. It had been a thing you did because you knew his name.

2. **On the warm kiln bricks** — A scarred yellow cat, three days on the perimeter wall, and then a decision.
   _a stray settling by the kiln_ (common)
   It drank the skimmed whey and went straight back up to the bricks it had been watching from, and it has been there every night since. Nobody in this house agreed to feed it. It never asked anyone to.

3. **The ledger closed in green** — An old account struck through in green chalk and tied shut with reed.
   _an account ruled through_ (common)
   The account is tied shut and the page underneath it is a colour no one has needed in years.

4. **A memory smoothed clean** — A slate given back to the rack with the writing taken out of it by water.
   _a slate returned unmarked_ (common)
   The marks go soft at the edges first and then all at once, the way a word leaves a face you are not looking at.

5. **The low gate left unlatched** — The bar resting on its bracket all night, and three people inside.
   _the bar resting on its bracket_ (rare)
   The latch is cold under your hand and has clearly been left alone for hours. Three sets of wet prints go in and none of them knocked.

### change

1. **The roads you never took** — Every mile of the crossing you worried about, and none of the miles you walked.
   _a shed weight on the mountain road_ (uncommon)
   The roads were not what made the pack heavy. You know that now, and you have not entirely forgiven the roads, and you have stopped packing for them.

2. **Two cups, always** — The second celadon cup comes off the rack before the kettle has finished, every time.
   _setting out two cups by instinct_ (common)
   It stopped being a courtesy some while ago and became a question you ask yourself, and the answer is always yes. There is a place set at your bench. There has been for weeks.

3. **Breath steadied at the boundary** — The panic at the ditch edge settling into a counted rhythm.
   _a measured rhythm behind the ribs_ (common)
   The panic comes up to the edge of the ditch and stops there, because you have counted past it.

4. **Hands calloused to the thorn** — Palms gone to horn-grain, so a blackthorn shoot can be gripped without opening.
   _yellow horn-grain on the palms_ (common)
   The horn-grain on the palms is thick enough that the sap has to be wiped off rather than washed out.

5. **Tolerance for cold limestone** — A body that will sit still on cold stone through another person's grief.
   _a heavy stillness on the stone_ (rare)
   Somebody else has been in grief for hours and you have stayed on the stone the whole time. You sat through a whole watch on that stone and your back never once complained.

### person

1. **Cor, the bell striker** — Still at the cedar pillar, mallet held in, giving the nod before the one note.
   _the one standing by the cedar pillar_ (uncommon)
   Cor stands perfectly still with the mallet held in, and the strike is the only fast thing that happens all day.

2. **Zhao the early courier** — He arrives while frost still silvers the gate latch, smelling of road dust and river mist.
   _a courier ahead of schedule_ (common)
   His pony's breath clouds the cold yard while he cuts the oiled twine off your parcel. He waves off the tea you offer, already tightening the girth strap to beat the morning packet boat downriver.

3. **Mire, the water tender** — Skimming the slow reach and watching the current at the sluice.
   _the one with the wicker scoop_ (common)
   Duckweed and twigs, lifted off the reach one at a time, all day, and the water is left clear.

4. **Maren, the seed gatherer** — Shaking blackthorn into a split basket, hands scratched to a pattern.
   _the one with the thorned fingers_ (common)
   The berries go into the split willow and the hedge along the north is not finished yet.

5. **The gate-hung, three owners deep** — He has mended the same gate for three households and will not take more for it.
   _an unasked mender_ (rare)
   He has mended the same gate-hung for three owners. He will do it again for a fourth, and the price has not moved in twenty years, and he says so the same way every time, which is to say once, and then not again. That gate has been mended by him for longer than it has hung on the same hinge.

### place

1. **The Twin Fountains terrace** — Two low basins and the straight worn line of flags walked between them.
   _flagstones between two low basins_ (uncommon)
   Neither basin is doing anything. You walk between them anyway, because the walking is the part that helps.

2. **The night market** — Oiled paper lanterns illuminate rows of noodle cauldrons and scrap-iron stalls along the west canal.
   _canal-side stalls after curfew_ (common)
   Oiled paper lanterns over noodle cauldrons and scrap-iron stalls, and the whole west canal eating standing up. In the morning the last cauldron is scraped for whoever has been working since before the first pot was lit.

3. **Wutai Shan** — The northern mountain revered as Mañjuśrī's seat.
   _a mountain of wisdom_ (common)
   Pilgrims climb past terraces where the sword is said to have been seen. The cold is part of the teaching, the way the climb is part of the arrival.

4. **The unlisted quay** — A hidden stone wharf tucked behind weeping willows where uninspected barges discharge their cargo before dawn.
   _a stone wharf behind the willows_ (uncommon)
   Cedar piles jut from the tidal mud, bound with greased cables that leave no gouge on an approaching hull. Small river smacks tie up with muffled oars and pay in unminted silver.

5. **The Bell Pavilion** — A low-hung bronze bell in four cedar posts, close enough to feel it arrive.
   _four posts and a low-hung bell_ (common)
   The mallet comes up off the rawhide and the sound stays in the four posts with you.
